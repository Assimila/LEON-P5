# LEON: Leveraging Earth Observation for Nature Finance

LEON (Leveraging Earth Observation for Nature Finance) takes a strategic, global and holistic approach to deliver a significant, measurable and long-lasting impact in scaling up nature finance. The project recognises that mobilising high-quality nature finance through fully integrating Earth Observation (EO) data into financial decision-making requires engagement along the full value chain, from EO providers to end-users.

A common challenge across both *financing green* and *greening finance* for nature is the lack of granular, high-quality geospatial data to assess risks, dependencies and impacts at the firm and asset level, design and issue robust biodiversity credits, and identify, monitor and verify the positive biodiversity impacts of investments. EO can play a critical role in unlocking progress through enhancing natural capital valuation.

LEON engages with the wider ecosystem of actors required to build the enabling environment for adoption, including governments, central banks and regulators, EO specialists, consultancies, financial, ESG and specialist nature-data providers, standard setters, ratings agencies, professional services, stock exchanges, real-economy firms and financial institutions. The project recognises the heterogeneous needs of different users, including differences in requirements for granularity, geographical scale and confidence levels. 

Six pilot studies address applications across the Nature Finance ecosystem:

- mining supply chains, with a focus on water quality
- agri-food supply chains, with a focus on land conversion
- nature-related financial risks and opportunities, including physical and transition risks
- biodiversity metrics and biodiversity credits
- biodiversity in natural-capital accounting and green net national income
- debt-for-nature swaps and sustainability-linked sovereign finance

While each pilot has a specific thematic focus, the general approach and outputs are consistent. The project focuses particularly on freely available satellite sensor data, including Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2 and Landsat, alongside other open-access datasets and spaceborne lidar. EO-derived data products are used to measure and characterise environmental variables that provide critical information on the state and trends of biodiversity. These variables are spatially and temporally scalable and can provide consistent information on landscapes for characterising and monitoring past and current conditions and supporting future planning and prediction.

Experimental datasets and indicators produced for each pilot are validated using reliable estimates of product accuracy and scientifically robust and statistically meaningful validation protocols. The project also considers data interpretation and use within decision-making contexts, with the aim of maximising the utility of the pilot-study outputs.

Through its pilot studies and engagement with Early Adopters and the wider Nature Finance community, LEON aims to bridge data and technology gaps to the integration, transfer and scalability of EO solutions and enhance communication across the different communities engaged in Nature Finance dialogue and action.
